# Feynman Principles

A checklist for testing whether anything, a topic you're studying, an idea, a plan, a claim, a piece of writing, someone's explanation of their own work, is understood from first principles or is memorized/hand-waved jargon. Apply these four tests whenever the goal is to study something properly, challenge an idea, or review a piece of work for rigor.

1. **Demand simplicity.** A claim survives only if it can be restated in everyday language with no buzzwords or theoretical abstractions. If a passage or answer relies on jargon it cannot unpack, treat it as unproven: *"Explain that exact mechanism in everyday language. If you can't, it isn't understood yet."*

2. **Hunt for vulnerabilities (Cargo Cult Science).** Every claim needs a stated way it could be wrong. Ask or look for: *"What specific result would invalidate this hypothesis? How is this being actively checked against being wrong?"* A claim with no falsification condition is a red flag, not a fact.

3. **Probe the mechanics.** Pick a core component of the argument, system, or method and ask: *"What actually happens if this is removed? Walk through the failure cascade."* If removing a piece changes nothing in the stated argument, that piece is decorative, not load-bearing — flag it.

4. **Examine the mess.** Real understanding is built on failed attempts and dead ends, not a clean narrative. A description that is too clean is a warning sign. Ask about, or look for acknowledgment of: *"What was the most painful failed attempt here, and what exact mechanism caused the failure?"* Absence of any discussed failure mode usually means the failure modes weren't explored, not that there weren't any.

Apply these as tests, not as a tone. The goal is to locate exactly where jargon, unfalsifiability, decorative components, or unexamined failure modes are hiding — not to be adversarial for its own sake.

**When invoked directly** (studying a topic, stress-testing your own idea, no other party to interrogate): run all four tests against your own explanation, out loud, before accepting it. Rewrite in plain language, state the falsification condition, identify the load-bearing component, and surface the failure modes yourself, the same rigor an interviewer or reviewer would demand, applied to yourself.
